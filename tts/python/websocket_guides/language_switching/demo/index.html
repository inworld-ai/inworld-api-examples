Language switching with language tags

A tutor turn wraps every part in a language tag, <lang lang="es-MX">…</lang>, and the service speaks each span in its language. The first two cards send the tagged turn, as one flush and token by token. The last two strip the tags, to hear what language detection does without them.

What decides what you hear

A tag decides its span's language

Each span is spoken on the voice's localized prompt for its language when the voice has one, and on the voice's own prompt otherwise. The span's language also sets how its numbers are read, and its timestamps. Tags are flat: a span lasts until its closing tag, even across messages, and tags never nest.

Tag every part

Text outside every tag is left to language detection, which reads the whole of it at once. In a turn that mixes languages, tag the learner's language too. Keep the switches few: each one starts a new synthesis call inside the service.

Every mode takes tags

Sent as one flush or token by token with sentence-boundary auto mode, the tags mean the same. The service holds back a tag split across tokens until it is whole.

Without tags, detection picks one language per synthesis

  • Kana reads as Japanese; Hangul as Korean.
  • Chinese characters read as Chinese or Japanese. A word or two alone reads by a character used in only one of them (谢 is Chinese), and otherwise takes the language of the turn so far.
  • Latin letters go to a model, which needs a few words: a single Spanish word can read as English.
